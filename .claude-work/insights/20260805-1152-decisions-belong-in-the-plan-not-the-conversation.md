## a decision that lives only in conversation dies at the context boundary

Writing a decision into `goal.md` and `impl.md` rather than just acting on it is
what makes "phase boundaries are session boundaries" workable — a decision that
exists only in conversation dies at the next context boundary, and the next
session would re-derive it (or silently contradict it).

The strike-through convention is doing real work here: after two amendments,
goal criterion 5 carries three states, and a reader can see the shape of the
argument rather than just its conclusion. Compare
[20260804-1022-superseding-beats-rewriting-a-stale-record] — same principle
applied to a document instead of a criterion.

The practical test: if the plan were the only thing that survived, would the
next reader make the same call? If not, the reasoning is still trapped in the
chat.
